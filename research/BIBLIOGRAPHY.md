## Runner & serving systems

- Kwon, W., Li, Z., Zhuang, S., Sheng, Y., Zheng, L., Yu, C. H., Gonzalez, J. E., Zhang, H., & Stoica, I. (2023). *Efficient Memory Management for Large Language Model Serving with PagedAttention.* SOSP '23, 611–626. arXiv:2309.06180. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180 — KV cache managed as OS-style paged memory (fixed blocks + block tables + copy-on-write sharing) removes fragmentation waste and lifts serving throughput 2–4×. Dossier: `papers/pagedattention-vllm.md`.
- Yu, G.-I., Jeong, J. S., Kim, G.-W., Kim, S., & Chun, B.-G. (2022). *Orca: A Distributed Serving System for Transformer-Based Generative Models.* OSDI '22, 521–538. https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/yu — iteration-level scheduling (continuous batching) plus selective batching. (No arXiv version.) Dossier: `papers/orca.md`.
- Zheng, L., Yin, L., Xie, Z., Sun, C., Huang, J., Yu, C. H., Cao, S., Kozyrakis, C., Stoica, I., Gonzalez, J. E., Barrett, C., & Sheng, Y. (2024). *SGLang: Efficient Execution of Structured Language Model Programs.* NeurIPS 37. arXiv:2312.07104. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07104 — RadixAttention keeps finished requests' KV in an LRU radix tree keyed by tokens, so shared prefixes are reused automatically, with cache-aware scheduling on top. Dossier: `papers/radixattention-sglang.md`.

## KV cache, attention, decoding

- Gim, I., Chen, G., Lee, S.-s., Sarda, N., Khandelwal, A., & Zhong, L. (2024). *Prompt Cache: Modular Attention Reuse for Low-Latency Inference.* MLSys 2024. arXiv:2311.04934. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04934 — reusable prompt segments declared as modules with precomputed attention states, spliced into new prompts to cut TTFT; largest gains on CPU. Dossier: `papers/prompt-cache.md`.
- Yao, J., Li, H., Liu, Y., Ray, S., Cheng, Y., Zhang, Q., Du, K., Lu, S., & Jiang, J. (2025). *CacheBlend: Fast Large Language Model Serving for RAG with Cached Knowledge Fusion.* EuroSys '25. DOI: 10.1145/3689031.3696098. arXiv:2405.16444. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16444 — reuses precomputed KV for chunks in any position and repairs cross-attention by recomputing only the highest-deviation ~15% of tokens. Dossier: `papers/cacheblend.md`.
- Xiao, G., Tian, Y., Chen, B., Han, S., & Lewis, M. (2024). *Efficient Streaming Language Models with Attention Sinks.* ICLR 2024. arXiv:2309.17453. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453 — permanently pinning ~4 initial tokens' KV (attention sinks) plus a sliding window gives constant-memory, stable streaming. Directly supports our character-anchor law. Dossier: `papers/streamingllm.md`.
- Zhang, Z., Sheng, Y., Zhou, T., Chen, T., Zheng, L., Cai, R., Song, Z., Tian, Y., Ré, C., Barrett, C., Wang, Z., & Chen, B. (2023). *H2O: Heavy-Hitter Oracle for Efficient Generative Inference of Large Language Models.* NeurIPS 36, 34661–34710. arXiv:2306.14048. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14048 — a fixed KV budget split between heavy-hitter tokens (by accumulated attention) and recent tokens preserves quality at ~20% of the full cache. Dossier: `papers/h2o.md`.

A model property, not a runtime policy. Dossier: `papers/gqa.md`.

### Attention kernels (wave 4)

### Scheduling & disaggregation (wave 4b)

- Agrawal, A., Kedia, N., Panwar, A., Mohan, J., Kwatra, N., Gulavani, B. S., Tumanov, A., & Ramjee, R. (2024). *Taming Throughput-Latency Tradeoff in LLM Inference with Sarathi-Serve.* OSDI '24, 117–134. arXiv:2403.02310. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02310 — chunked prefill + decode-maximal batching under a fixed token budget bounds every step's latency by the budget, not the longest prompt; up to 2.6× serving capacity (Mistral-7B, 1× A100) under P99-TBT SLOs. For a single-user runner: chunking doesn't shorten TTFT — it bounds the stall (Volmarr's 52.1 s prompt silence is this paper's pathology at its own scale). Dossier: `papers/sarathi-serve.md`.
- Zhong, Y., Liu, S., Chen, J., Hu, J., Zhu, Y., Liu, X., Jin, X., & Zhang, H. (2024). *DistServe: Disaggregating Prefill and Decoding for Goodput-optimized Large Language Model Serving.* OSDI '24, 193–210. arXiv:2401.09670. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09670 — phases disaggregated onto separate pools with searched parallelism, bandwidth-aware KV handoff; up to 7.4× more requests or 12.6× tighter SLOs (v3/OSDI figures; arXiv v1 reported 4.48×/10.2× — both recorded in the dossier). Mechanism does not apply at one GPU (its own §7); the TTFT/TPOT budget discipline does. Dossier: `papers/distserve.md`.
- Patel, P., Choukse, E., Zhang, C., Shah, A., Goiri, Í., Maleki, S., & Bianchini, R. (2024). *Splitwise: Efficient Generative LLM Inference Using Phase Splitting.* ISCA 2024, 118–132. DOI: 10.1109/ISCA59077.2024.00019. arXiv:2311.18677. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18677 — characterisation first: prefill is compute-bound, decode memory-bound (H100's 3.43× A100 compute buys only ~0.70× its TBT); phase-split pools reach 1.4× throughput at 20% lower cost on production traces. Dossier: `papers/splitwise.md`.

## Bibliography additions — Papers Wave 9 (sampling & decoding)

Ollama Research Forge, OR-3 Paper Sweep, Wave 9. Accessed 2026-11-15. All entries verified against arXiv / venue records on that date.

- Nguyen, M. N., Baker, A., Neo, C., Roush, A., Kirsch, A., & Shwartz-Ziv, R. (2025). *Turning Up the Heat: Min-p Sampling for Creative and Coherent LLM Outputs.* Proceedings of the 2025 International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2025) — oral presentation. arXiv:2407.01082. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01082 — Essence: replace top-p's fixed cumulative-mass threshold with a floor denominated in the model's own confidence — keep only tokens with probability at least $p_{\text{base}} \times p_{\max}$ — so the pool narrows automatically when the model is certain and widens when it is torn, which is why its benchmark advantage in the paper's tables is a collapse-resistance effect at high temperature (Mistral 7B GPQA at $\tau=3.0$: min-p 24.55% vs. top-p 0.46%) rather than a gain at ordinary settings. Born as a community sampler before it was a paper, it is now the persona-chat stack's default truncator (llama.cpp chain position: last filter before XTC and temperature; Ollama exposes `min_p` but defaults it off) — with a standing caveat: a 2025 re-examination (arXiv:2506.13681) disputes the human-evaluation and benchmark-superiority claims, so the corpus banks the mechanism as proven and the effect sizes as contested. - Basu, S., Ramachandran, G. S., Keskar, N. S., & Varshney, L. R. - Dong, Y., Ruan, C. F., Cai, Y., Lai, R., Xu, Z., Zhao, Y., & Chen, T. (2025). *XGrammar: Flexible and Efficient Structured Generation Engine for Large Language Models.* Proceedings of the 8th Conference on Machine Learning and Systems (MLSys 2025), Santa Clara, CA, USA. arXiv:2411.15100. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15100 — Essence: full context-free-grammar constrained decoding made near-free by refusing to interpret the grammar over the whole vocabulary at runtime — the CFG is compiled to a byte-level pushdown automaton (the stack is what JSON's arbitrary nesting requires and no FSM has), and the vocabulary is split by what a token's validation actually needs: context-independent tokens, decidable from the stack-top node alone, are precomputed into an adaptive token mask cache (accept-heavy nodes store only their short rejected list, reject-heavy nodes only their short accepted list — 160 MB → 0.46 MB for Llama-3.1 + JSON), while context-dependent tokens, which need the whole stack, are under 1% of the vocabulary (1,134 of 128k) and are cut a further 90% (to 120) by context expansion against precomputed expanded-suffix automata; the residue is checked live against a persistent execution stack that makes branching and rollback pointer operations — the property that lets automaton state fork and rewind transactionally with speculative draft trees — and mask generation is overlapped with the GPU forward pass so the constraint synchronises only at sampling. As reported: under 40 µs/token mask latency for JSON Schema and CFG (JSON), up to 100× mask-generation and up to 80× end-to-end speedups over Outlines v1.0 / llama.cpp b3998 / lm-format-enforcer baselines, TPOT essentially unchanged with the engine enabled (6.2 → 6.3 ms at batch 1), and syntactic correctness 62% → 100% (function calling) and 80% → 100% (XML) — the correctness standard being distribution fidelity: invalid logits to −∞, relative probabilities of valid tokens preserved, renormalisation over the valid set only.
- Willard, B. T., & Louf, R. (2023). *Efficient Guided Generation for Large Language Models.* arXiv preprint. arXiv:2307.09702. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09702 — Essence: the founding index design for guided generation, implemented in the open-source library Outlines — generation is reformulated as transitions of a finite-state machine carried alongside the token stream, and the per-step mask is turned from an $O(N)$ scan of the vocabulary (plus a re-parse of the growing prefix) into an $O(1)$-average lookup by paying the vocabulary cost once: every vocabulary string is walked through the regex's FSM from every state that could consume its first character, building the index $\sigma : Q \to \mathcal{P}(V)$ from FSM states to the token sets accepted from them, so sampling becomes mask ($\tilde{\alpha} = m \odot \alpha$, the model's distribution conditioned on the valid set and renormalised over it), sample, advance the machine — with guidance startable and stoppable mid-sequence and the whole method model-agnostic over next-token logits. The paper extends the construction in sketch to CFGs via LALR(1) parsing, identifying the hard case honestly — partial lexing, where a sampled token ends mid-terminal (`"def f"` is not yet a finished `NAME`) — and keying the parser index as a trie over stack values, the point past which XGrammar's split-and-cache design takes over; its one head-to-head (GPT-2, regex task, vs. the Guidance library's per-step re-matching) shows near-flat vs. steeply growing runtime in output length, and its naively built Python-grammar index is ~50 MB. The project it founded (now maintained by .txt / dottxt-ai) later became a front end over pluggable engines — its own `outlines_core`, XGrammar, and llguidance — which is the field's own synthesis of the two papers in this wave: this one's interface, the companion's engine for the hardest grammars.
