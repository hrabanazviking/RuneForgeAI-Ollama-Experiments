## 1. Method note — how ideas were scored

Each idea was scored on four axes, for the target workload above and no other:

1. **Expected gain for the 6 GB single-user long-chat workload.** Prefill avoided, context bought, decode multiplied, or a failure mode removed — in that workload's units (tokens of usable context, seconds of prefill per turn, tok/s at batch 1), never in the source's native units (fleet throughput, batch-64 rates) without translation. Where the corpus's own worked case exists (kv-cache-design §7.2), it governs over any paper headline.
2. **Cost.** Engineering depth (policy flag vs allocator surgery vs new kernels), resident-memory cost on a card that has none to spare, and operational cost (calibration artefacts, per-model validation, training pipelines).
3. **Risk.** Correctness risk first (silent quality loss outranks a crash — a crash is at least honest), then portability risk across arbitrary community GGUFs, then maintenance risk.
4. **Evidence strength**, graded per entry:
   - **A** — measured in a peer-reviewed paper's own evaluation, or in our own campaign records (Aesir gates, MimirAlloc gates), with hardware and workload stated.
   - **B** — reported by a project (docs, release notes, project benchmarks) without independent paper-grade evaluation in the corpus.
   - **C** — reasoned inference: our translation of a measured mechanism to this workload, a dossier's labelled judgement estimate, or an adaptation the source does not itself make. **C is marked honestly wherever the gain — not just the mechanism — is unmeasured on anything like our target.** Several high ranks are C-gain/A-mechanism; the entry says which.

Two corpus disciplines were applied to every number: no number travels without its context (machine, model, precision, workload), and a claim is never stated stronger than its source states it. Where two dossiers give the same idea in different strengths (e.g. prefix reuse in Ollama §9 A2, vLLM §9.2, SGLang §9.1), the ledger carries one entry at the strongest well-evidenced form and names the others as lineage.

**Ranking principle:** correctness and residency foundations first (a fast runner that beheads the persona is worth nothing), then the memory arithmetic that decides what fits, then decode speed, then refinements whose value is real but conditional. Dependencies break ties: an idea that unblocks three others outranks a slightly larger standalone win.

## 3. The ledger — entries

### #1 — Resident session object with token-level residency
- **Source:** `runners/ollama.md` §9 A1; `aesir/aesir-ideas-for-ollama.md` §2.1–§2.2, §3.1 (HS-6, HS-3); `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.6 lineage.
- **Mechanism:** The conversation becomes a server-side session object that owns its token stream and KV across turns; clients send only the new turn. The session tracks exactly which tokens have KV committed, token by token — generated tokens are committed the moment they are produced — and each request reuses the longest exact token prefix, with the final committed token always re-evaluated as the price of exactness.
- **Expected gain:** Per-turn prefill falls from O(history) to O(new tokens) *guaranteed*, not conditionally on slot luck. Aesir's recorded baseline disease is Ollama-via-Voxta re-evaluating 14,712 tokens in 52.1 s per turn; Aesir's own small proof (HS-3) reused 46 tokens, turn 2 at 610 ms vs 825 ms. The large-chat magnitude on our card is inference, not measurement — hence the split grade.
- **Cost/risk:** The deepest product change in the ledger: session expiry, ownership, crash recovery. Mitigation from the Ollama dossier: sessions are an optimisation layer *over* the stateless API — the client still owns the message log and can replay to rebuild.
- **Evidence: A** for the mechanism and the seam-loss elimination (Aesir gates, bit-exact records); **C** for the size of the win on purplelongship.
- **Home:** anchor/residency — the foundation slice group; #5, #7, #10, #14 all presuppose it.

### #2 — Pinned, non-truncatable anchor segment
- **Source:** `runners/ollama.md` §9 B1 (and §4.3, the two-layer truncation finding); `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.2; `papers/streamingllm.md` §7a; `memory/kv-cache-design.md` Rule 5.
- **Mechanism:** A request is `[anchor][history][new]`, with the anchor (persona/system) declared as a distinct pinned segment. Truncation and shifting apply to history only, oldest-first; `num_keep` becomes "the whole anchor", not 4 tokens. Stock Ollama's layer-2 shift preserves 4–5 head tokens and frees ~half the window on first shift — at `num_ctx` 4096 a 12,000-token prompt reaches the model as ~5 + ~2,046 tokens — which is the failure this entry abolishes.
- **Expected gain:** Not a speed number: the catastrophic, silent failure mode — a long conversation amputating the persona to 5 tokens — becomes structurally impossible. The corpus's clearest demonstrated harm, removed by policy.
- **Cost/risk:** A pinned anchor permanently consumes window; cap it (the Ollama dossier suggests ≤25% of window) and reject oversize anchors loudly at session creation.
- **Evidence: A** — the failure is source-verified in Ollama's own code at named tags; StreamingLLM's sink measurements (below, #9) independently show why head eviction collapses quality.
- **Home:** anchor/residency — ship with #6; it is cheap (policy, not kernels) and is Volmarr's law, not an optimisation.

### #3 — q8_0 KV by default on small cards, K/V separate, per-model
- **Source:** `runners/ollama.md` §9 C1 (§4.5, §5.5); `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.3; `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §7.1 Step 3, §7.2 worked case, Rule 4.
- **Mechanism:** Below a VRAM threshold, default the KV cache to q8_0 instead of f16, with K and V types set independently (K is empirically the more sensitive) and a per-model escape hatch to f16. Pure policy over cache types Ollama already ships; requires Flash Attention on the backend, and the *effective* type in force must be reported, including any coupling surprise.
- **Expected gain:** The corpus's worked case (Llama-3.1-8B class, Q4_K_M weights ≈4.9 GiB, 614 MiB KV budget on the 6 GiB card after weights and a 512 MiB planning reserve): f16 affords **4,912 tokens** — failing even an 8K minimum — while q8_0 affords **9,246**, so the build ships 8,192 with ~13% headroom. At 32K the saving is 4 GiB → ~2.1 GiB. q4_0 (~17,465 tokens) is an explicitly labelled experiment only: its long-context quality cost is documented as noticeable.
- **Cost/risk:** Quality risk grows with grouped-query-head count and context length; validate per model family (#34) and label each precision with its validated context range. Changing cache type invalidates saved sessions (#10's envelope must record it).
- **Evidence: A** — arithmetic from verified formulas plus the project's own characterisation of q8_0's cost as usually unnoticeable; the worked case is the corpus's own derivation, not a vendor claim.
- **Home:** KV/memory — the cheapest large win in the ledger; do before any eviction scoring (#9), because scoring operates on the cache this entry sizes.

### #4 — Block (paged) KV allocation in one pre-sized arena
- **Source:** `papers/pagedattention-vllm.md`; `runners/vllm.md` §9.1; `runners/ollama.md` §9 C2; `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §2, §4.1, Rule 2; `memory/mimiralloc-our-own-proven-work.md` §5 (the negative allocator evidence).
- **Mechanism:** Pre-size one KV pool at load; inside it, hand out fixed-size blocks (default 16 tokens per kv-cache-design; the vLLM dossier argues 32–64 wastes negligibly at single-sequence scale and cuts table work — a build-time constant to sweep, see Open Questions) through a per-sequence block table with reference counts and a free list. A session grows one block at a time instead of reserving window × slots up front. No per-block host allocation, ever: our own MimirAlloc record is the standing proof that a general allocator is the wrong tool for this shape (it lost all three KV-shaped probes, Z-115).
- **Expected gain:** Memory honesty rather than throughput: prior systems used only 20.4%–38.2% of allocated KV for actual token states (PagedAttention paper's profiling, datacentre traces). The Ollama dossier's judgement estimate for our shape is 2–4× effective context capacity at low concurrency — one real 12–16K session instead of four reserved 4K slots. A cheaper approximation (start small, grow the runner preserving KV via #10) captures much of it if full surgery must wait.
- **Cost/risk:** The deepest runner surgery in the top five; every attention path must read through the table. Kernel complexity is the risk, mitigated by a build-time-constant block size and a plain slice-of-ints table.
- **Evidence: A** for the mechanism (SOSP '23, exact — no accuracy change); **C** for the 2–4× capacity estimate on our workload.
- **Home:** KV/memory — foundation; #5, #9, #10, #11, #23 all build on it.

### #5 — Hash-chained / radix prefix cache: persona trunk + branches
- **Source:** `runners/sglang.md` §9.1; `papers/radixattention-sglang.md`; `runners/vllm.md` §9.2; `runners/ollama.md` §9 A2; `runners/mlx.md` S-2.
- **Mechanism:** Finished prefixes stay resident, indexed either as hash-chained blocks (parent hash + tokens + model/quant/precision + scope) or as a token-keyed radix tree — the tree form additionally captures partial overlap (same character, different scenario). The persona/system trunk is inserted once and shared by reference count across sessions and branches; a swipe or regenerate forks at a node and pays only for its suffix (copy-on-write on the boundary block); eviction is LRU over unreferenced leaves only, with the live session's trunk and tip pinned.
- **Expected gain:** In the independent consumer-hardware measurement cited by the SGLang dossier, a 94%-cached prompt cut TTFT ~13× on a laptop-class chip and ~5× on an RTX 3090; an MLX field case shows a cached turn at ~1.4 s vs ~29.5 s uncached. Fleet hit rates of 50%–99% are paper-reported on datacentre workloads and are *not* the expectation here; the local expectation is the shape — later-turn TTFT falls by roughly the cached fraction. Memory saving is equally real: N branches share one trunk instead of N copies.
- **Cost/risk:** The largest single engineering item after #4: node splitting, reference counts, leaf eviction, and ruthless invalidation on model/quant/template change (stale KV is wrong KV). Subtle corruption, not crashes, is the failure mode — budget parity tests.
- **Evidence: A** for the mechanism (NeurIPS '24 paper + production reports); **B** for the consumer-hardware TTFT figures (one independent author's measurements).
- **Home:** anchor/residency, on top of #4. Prefix caching before tiering (#10/#11): there is nothing to tier until prefixes are retained objects.

### #6 — Loud disclosure of every truncation, eviction and reuse count
- **Source:** `runners/ollama.md` §9 B2; `memory/kv-cache-design.md` Rules 5–6; `aesir/aesir-ideas-for-ollama.md` §2.4, T11.
- **Mechanism:** Every response carries structured disclosure: tokens reused vs evaluated, segments/tokens dropped by any truncation or eviction, the effective window and cache precision in force. For pinned-anchor sessions, truncation/shift defaults to *error*, not silent repair — on every API route, including compatibility routes (extension field or headers where the dialect requires it). The serve also advertises which memory properties actually hold per session (Aesir's `exact_prefix_reuse` / `session_residency` / `memory_injection` pattern), truthfully or not at all.
- **Expected gain:** Converts the corpus's central integrity failure — fluent HTTP 200 answers computed from prompts the client did not send — into observable, alertable events. No speed claim; this is what makes every other entry's behaviour verifiable in production.
- **Cost/risk:** Can break clients that assume any 200-shaped success; gate the error-default behind session type, keep disclosure universal.
- **Evidence: B/C** — the silence is source-verified (Ollama dossier §4.3); the disclosure contract is our design, reasoned from it.
- **Home:** robustness — pair with #2 from the first build slice; retrofitting observability later is how it never happens.

### #7 — Post-history anchor slot (composed, never stored)
- **Source:** `aesir/aesir-ideas-for-ollama.md` §2.3 (HS-5, banked `8f98b2a5`); the same pattern set in Voxta, 2026-11-14 (our records).
- **Mechanism:** A serve-level anchor block (config default + per-request override) is rendered *after* the history at prompt-assembly time, every turn, and is never written into stored history or the committed token stream. Because it is composed rather than stored, history growth cannot push it out, and prefix reuse (#5) still covers the whole history in front of it.
- **Expected gain:** The anchor is the nearest thing to the generation point and immune to the window entirely — the complement to #2's pinned head for designs (like our Voxta work) that need the character prompt at the end. Cost is explicit: `len(anchorTokens)` re-evaluated per turn.
- **Cost/risk:** The per-turn re-evaluation tax is real for a long anchor; keep the anchor short and fixed, and budget it in the open (#12). An anchor that changes per turn also breaks the discipline of #13 for anything after it — there is nothing after it, which is the point.
- **Evidence: A** — banked and proven in our own Aesir serve line (HS-5), with the unpushable property as its recorded design intent.
- **Home:** anchor/residency — after #1 (it needs the session's composition layer), alongside #2.

### #8 — Whole-turn summarise-then-evict middle management
- **Source:** `runners/ollama.md` §9 B3; `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.2 (d); `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §4.2 composition judgement.
- **Mechanism:** When history must shrink, drop or compress *whole turns* (user/assistant pairs), never partial turns and never tokens from inside a turn or adjacent to the anchor. Preferred form: summarise the dropped span into a short recap segment inserted after the anchor (itself pinned, second tier), accepting one summarisation pass as the price of coherence. Token-level middle excision (llama.cpp shift style) is reserved for raw completion mode, where it was born.
- **Expected gain:** Degradation becomes graceful and semantically coherent — older turns fade; persona and current exchange remain intact. No corpus measurement quantifies chat quality under this policy vs raw discard; the gain is a correctness-of-degradation claim.
- **Cost/risk:** Whole-turn units lose information in larger chunks than token eviction, and summarisation costs a generation pass plus a recap-prefill. The recap can itself drift from the dropped facts — it is a memory aid, not a record (see #9's honesty requirement).
- **Evidence: C** — reasoned composition of two dossiers' prescriptions; neither measures it.
- **Home:** accuracy — the policy layer above #9's mechanism; the two must be designed together so scoring never splits a turn.

### #9 — Eviction composition: pinned head + scored middle + recent window
- **Source:** `papers/streamingllm.md`; `papers/h2o.md`; `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §4.2, §7.1 Step 6, Rule 5.
- **Mechanism:** Three pools, three rules, one declared composition. (i) Pinned head: the persona block plus the first few tokens forever — StreamingLLM's ablation found 4 sink tokens reach essentially full quality on Llama-2-7B, and evicting the head collapses perplexity (Llama-2-13B window attention 5158 vs 5.40 sink+window on PG-19). (ii) Scored middle: heavy-hitter retention by accumulated attention (H2O), evicting the lowest-scored non-recent token at block granularity. (iii) Recent window: never evicted, protecting tokens that have not yet had the chance to prove themselves. Every eviction is reported (#6).
- **Expected gain:** H2O at a 20% cache budget stayed within ~2% perplexity of full cache on its reported suites where recency-only policies degraded sharply on recall tasks (paper's measurements, A100/FlexGen implementation, OPT/LLaMA/GPT-NeoX). For our workload the honest translation is: a much longer *usable* conversation at the resident cache #3 sizes — not a longer *remembered* one. Evicted detail is gone permanently and the model will confabulate rather than admit it; pair with #8's recap/external memory for facts.
- **Cost/risk:** The integration cost is attention-score accumulation: fused attention kernels deliberately do not materialise attention weights. A per-step reduction over the newest query's weights is O(context) and small; if the runner cannot do even that, a sampled-steps degraded variant must be labelled as such and quality-tested. Irreversibility is intrinsic.
- **Evidence: A** for both component policies (ICLR '24; NeurIPS '23); **C** for the three-pool composition as a chat policy (the composition is the corpus's synthesis, kv-cache-design §4.2).
- **Home:** KV/memory — after #3 (precision first: scoring a cache you are about to halve re-scores everything) and after #4 (eviction is at block granularity).

### #10 — Session persistence to disk/RAM with a validity envelope
- **Source:** `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.1 (§3.4); `runners/ollama.md` §9 A3; `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §4.4, §7.1 Step 7; `runners/sglang.md` §9.1 (HiCache tiering, miniaturised).
- **Mechanism:** Serialise a named session's stable-prefix KV to fast local storage at unload, on idle-timeout, and at branch points; reload instead of re-evaluating on reopen. The file carries its own validity envelope — model identity (GGUF hash + quant + build), context size, cache precision, tokeniser/template version, token count, checksum — and refuses loudly and distinctly on mismatch, where upstream returns one generic 400. Store quantised (q8_0 halves the file). A session file *is* the conversation in recoverable form and inherits the conversation's privacy handling.
- **Expected gain:** Measured on a CPU-only VM (Qwen3-1.7B, 7,289-token prompt): restore **2.14 s vs 160.7 s** cold prefill; a second measurement put a 5K-token chat at ~7× (9.9 s → 1.4 s). On GPU hardware the absolutes shrink but file-read vs compute still favours restore for long prefixes; returning-conversation TTFT becomes near-constant.
- **Cost/risk:** Disk: the measured 7.3K-token session was 836 MB for one small model; a 32K-context 8B session at f16 is ~4 GiB by the §5.5 arithmetic. Needs an eviction policy and the discipline not to save short chats whose cold prefill is already fast.
- **Evidence: A** — community measurements reported and dissected in two dossiers; the envelope design is ours (C for that part).
- **Home:** anchor/residency — the persistence tier of #5; sequence strictly after prefix caching exists.

### #11 — Decouple model lifetime from session lifetime; spill idle, never running
- **Source:** `runners/ollama.md` §9 A4; `runners/vllm.md` §9.4; `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §4.3.
- **Mechanism:** Hold session KV in system RAM (or in #10's store) independently of weight residency, so evicting a model for a second model does not destroy the first model's sessions. Preemption policy, laptop form: never preempt the *running* sequence; spill *idle* sessions to RAM (llama.cpp's bounded RAM-cache shape), then to disk at checkpoints; keep recompute as the always-working fallback for short spans only, and make any preemption of the live conversation visible ("context was compacted"), never silent.
- **Expected gain:** Multi-model hosts (chat + embeddings + a second persona) stop thrashing each other's context; the product degrades understandably instead of OOMing or re-prefilling a 32K conversation (minutes on a laptop) because a datacentre cost model said recompute beats swap. That datacentre result inverts locally: host RAM is plentiful relative to VRAM and restore beats recompute for long prefixes (#10's numbers).
- **Cost/risk:** RAM pressure and a scheduler that must reason about two resources (weights, sessions). The spill tier is real engineering: format, integrity checks, eviction, load-on-demand. Start cheap — spill whole turns aligned to block boundaries, keyed by #5's hash chain.
- **Evidence: B/C** — vLLM's recompute-only V1 policy and llama.cpp's RAM cache are project-reported; the inversion argument for laptops is the corpus's reasoning, supported by #10's measurements.
- **Home:** KV/memory — with #10 as one persistence policy at two speeds.

### #12 — Budget from `kv_rate` + measured placement, decisions exposed
- **Source:** `memory/kv-cache-design.md` Rule 1, §7.1 Steps 1–2; `runners/ollama.md` §9 C4, C5 (§5.4–§5.5); `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.5 (`--fit`); `runners/vllm.md` §9.5 (profiling run).
- **Mechanism:** Compute bytes-per-token from model metadata (attention layers × KV heads × head dim × precision) at load — never from parameter count or advertised context — and show the crossover context beside any `num_ctx` control. Replace heuristic memory estimation with pre-load measurement of the exact requirement (weights + cache + scratch) per (model, window, slots, cache type), cached and self-invalidating on driver/backend change; consult the engine's fit logic rather than reinventing it, then *show the user what was fitted* (layers offloaded, context chosen, cache types) with pinnable overrides. Ship the coherent small-card profile as one named decision: one slot, quantised KV, mmap on, session-scoped pinning, window derived from the arithmetic for the specific model.
- **Expected gain:** The project's own published example of measured placement gained ~64% generation speed from packing alone (12B model, 128K context, single RTX 4090: 52.02 → 85.54 tok/s). On 6 GB the same precision decides whether the last layers spill to CPU — the steepest speed cliff in the system — so the local gain is "the cliff, avoided or knowingly accepted" (#3's sibling rule: buy VRAM back with cache precision, then a smaller weight quant, before accepting layer spill).
- **Cost/risk:** Measurement must be per-configuration and cached; `--fit`-class behaviour drifts between upstream builds, so pin the build and snapshot-test fitted outputs per upgrade.
- **Evidence: A/B** — the arithmetic is exact; the packing gain is project-reported (B) on different hardware.
- **Home:** KV/memory — design-time companion to #3; its UI half (show the arithmetic) is part of #6's honesty contract.

### #13 — Byte-stable prompt rendering as a caching requirement
- **Source:** `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.6; `runners/sglang.md` §9.1 (prompt-assembly discipline); `runners/ollama.md` §9 D1; `papers/radixattention-sglang.md` §7a.
- **Mechanism:** Treat byte-identical prefix rendering as a requirement on the chat-template layer, not a hope: anchor first and byte-stable; history after, turn-aligned; volatility last. No timestamps, session ids, rotating tips or retrieval-score-ordered blocks ahead of or inside the stable prefix; clients keep append-only discipline (corrections in new turns, never mutating an early message). Add a `tokens_cached` regression check to telemetry — if it trends to zero, the template broke, and that is a bug report, not a mystery.
- **Expected gain:** Derived from the reuse mechanics: with a 7K-token history and a 200-token new turn, per-turn prefill is ~200 tokens instead of ~7,200 — every turn, for free. This entry is what makes #1 and #5 pay in practice rather than in principle.
- **Cost/risk:** Template rigidity: any feature wanting per-turn prompt variation must live after the stable prefix or it silently destroys the cache — and without the telemetry check, nothing will say so.
- **Evidence: A/C** — the mechanism is exact (A); the per-turn token counts are arithmetic from stated sizes (derived, labelled as such in the source dossier).
- **Home:** speed (it is the cheapest speed in the ledger) — enforce from the first session slice; it constrains #14's design.

### #14 — Stable-order, whole-block memory injection under a token budget
- **Source:** `aesir/aesir-ideas-for-ollama.md` §3.3 (HS-7, banked `fd53cc8c`).
- **Mechanism:** Memory injected into a prompt (recall, lore, world-info) arrives in the same order every turn — sorted by a fixed key (priority, then stable id; never by recency or retrieval score, which change turn to turn) — admitted in whole blocks or not at all, against a %-of-context budget, dropping the lowest-priority whole block when over budget rather than truncating mid-block. Per-turn reuse counters are logged so churn is visible.
- **Expected gain:** Protects #5/#13: any reordering or partial admission changes the token prefix and destroys the reuse the session exists to provide. The gain is the preservation of the prefix-cache win in exactly the workload (retrieval-augmented character chat) most likely to lose it.
- **Cost/risk:** Whole-block admission wastes the partial space a truncated block could have used; the budget discipline is the product decision. Retrieval quality may prefer score order — the contract deliberately subordinates it to stability.
- **Evidence: A** — banked in our own Aesir serve line with its contract recorded (fixed order, budgeted whole-block admission, reuse counters); the Ollama-side magnitude is unmeasured.
- **Home:** accuracy — design with #8; both are "how memory enters and leaves without lying about it".

### #15 — N-gram / prompt-lookup speculation as the default accelerator
- **Source:** `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.4; `papers/speculative-decoding.md` §7a; `papers/lookahead-decoding.md` §7a.
- **Mechanism:** Propose continuations by matching recent token n-grams against the prompt and the text generated so far (llama.cpp's `ngram-simple`/`ngram-cache` lineage), and verify them with the target model under the standard lossless rule. No second model, no trained heads, no extra VRAM — the pool is megabytes of hash/cache structure. Expose the acceptance rate in UI/logs: it is the only honest signal of whether speculation is helping *this* conversation.
- **Expected gain:** The family's floor is measured: a trivial bigram drafter (acceptance α ≈ 0.20, cost ratio c ≈ 0) bought **1.25×** on T5-XXL in Leviathan et al.'s experiments. Character dialogue is unusually repetitive in the favourable way (names, forms of address, recurring phrases, scene markers), so chat acceptance should sit above generic prose — that expectation is inference, and the dossier is explicit that n-gram gains are *unmeasured for character chat specifically*. Worst case is ~1.0× plus lookup cost; the output distribution is preserved exactly either way.
- **Cost/risk:** Reported `--parallel 1` restriction (unverified against source — Open Questions) means speculation is a single-user feature: disable it cleanly when a second slot is in use, never silently. Greedy-vs-probabilistic semantics must be pinned per deployment.
- **Evidence: A** for the lossless framework and the bigram floor (ICML '23); **C** for the chat-specific upside.
- **Home:** speed — the first speculation slice; #26–#28 are conditional upgrades from this baseline, never replacements for having it.

### #16 — Attention-pattern-aware KV allocation (per-layer real cost)
- **Source:** `runners/ollama.md` §9 C3 (§4.5); `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §2.4.
- **Mechanism:** Charge each layer's cache according to its real attention pattern — full vs sliding-window vs chunked — instead of window × layers uniformly. Ollama's own new-engine work demonstrated the shape for Gemma-class sliding windows: faithful per-layer accounting converts directly into a larger usable window or more concurrency on the same memory.
- **Expected gain:** Model-dependent; for sliding-window-heavy architectures the saving is a large fraction of nominal cache at long windows — potentially the difference between fitting and not fitting at 16K on 6 GB. No honest single number exists without naming the model.
- **Cost/risk:** Requires per-architecture correctness; an approximately-right attention implementation degrades output progressively (the new-engine announcement's own warning). Implement per model family with parity tests (#34), or not at all.
- **Evidence: B** — project-reported (Ollama's new-engine announcement as read in the dossier); no paper-grade evaluation in the corpus.
- **Home:** KV/memory — after #4 (blocks make per-layer quotas expressible).

### #17 — Window as capacity, not runner identity (no reload thrash)
- **Source:** `runners/ollama.md` §9 D4 (§3.4); §9 C5.
- **Mechanism:** One runner allocated at the session's declared maximum serves all smaller requests; differing sampler options never force a reload (they are per-request already — keep them that way); differing windows queue onto the resident runner instead of replacing it. Pair with #4's grow-on-demand allocation so holding the maximum window does not mean paying for it idly.
- **Expected gain:** Eliminates multi-second reload stalls in mixed-client deployments entirely (dossier's claim, mechanism-level). On a single-client companion setup the win is smaller but the failure it removes — a second tool's request evicting the chat's runner mid-conversation — is exactly the kind users remember.
- **Cost/risk:** The resident runner holds the maximum window's reservation unless #4 lands; sequence after #4 or accept the interim cost knowingly.
- **Evidence: B/C** — the reload-on-options-change behaviour is source-verified in the Ollama dossier; the stall elimination is reasoned from it.
- **Home:** robustness.

### #18 — Chunked prefill with progress and cancellation
- **Source:** `runners/vllm.md` §9.3; `runners/mlx.md` S-5; `papers/orca.md` §7a (step-loop shape).
- **Mechanism:** Cap new tokens per engine step (512–2048 as the local starting range; auto-size the chunk to current headroom on tight budgets); a long prefill is split across steps, yielding to the event loop and a cancel check between slices, with streamed progress ("prefilling… 12,400 / 96,000 tokens"). The attention implementation must support attending over a partially prefilled prefix with correct position offsets — the same machinery #5's suffix prefill needs.
- **Expected gain:** Zero for raw speed and stated as such in the source: chunked prefill does not shorten the request's own TTFT. The gain is that a long paste or document ingest converts from "the app hung" into a cancellable progress bar — on a laptop, often the difference between a kept user and a lost one.
- **Cost/risk:** Low; the risk is a subtle position/attention-mask bug across chunk boundaries. Acceptance test from the dossier: a long prompt computed chunked vs whole must produce identical logits.
- **Evidence: B/C** — standard in vLLM V1 (project-reported); the local sizing is our translation.
- **Home:** robustness — cheap; schedule any time after the step loop exists.

### #19 — Asymmetric KV quantisation: K per-channel, V per-token, FP16 residual
- **Source:** `papers/kivi.md`; `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §5.2.
- **Mechanism:** Keys and values have opposite outlier geometry, so quantise them along opposite axes: keys per-channel (grouped along the token axis, so outlier channels get their own scale), values per-token (so each token's error stays its own). New K/V land in a full-precision residual window (paper defaults: group 32, residual 128); when it fills, its oldest block is quantised into the grouped body. Attention runs in two tiles — fused dequantise-in-matmul over the grouped part, ordinary path over the residual — concatenated before softmax. The residual is load-bearing, not overhead: fake-quantising everything drops Llama-2-7B GSM8K from 13.50 to 5.76, vs 12.74 for KIVI-2 with its residual.
- **Expected gain:** Paper-measured (A100-80GB, HuggingFace implementation): KIVI-2 at 2.6× less peak memory including weights and near-baseline quality on its suites (Llama-2-7B LongBench average 44.52 FP16 vs 44.27 KIVI-2). Corpus arithmetic for our shape: an 8K context's ~1 GiB FP16 cache (7B GQA worked example) falls to roughly 150–200 MB including residual and scales. This is the tier *beyond* #3 — q8_0 first, this when 8K is not enough.
- **Cost/risk:** A llama.cpp-class kernel project, not a flag flip — Ollama's existing uniform cache types are not this scheme. Evaluation ceiling: LongBench capped at 4,096 tokens for most models in the paper; nothing validates 2-bit at 32K+. Falcon/MQA is the warning label: already head-compressed caches have less headroom, and the paper's own fix is 4-bit. Default per model by KV-head structure, gated by #34.
- **Evidence: A** — ICML '24, with the axis ablation stated brutally (any V-per-channel configuration collapses to ~2.8 / ~0.3–0.7 on its CoQA/TruthfulQA fake-quant tests).
- **Home:** KV/memory — the second precision tier, after #3 is validated per model.

### #20 — Precision follows importance: sink/head FP16, 1% outlier sparing
- **Source:** `papers/kvquant.md`; `runners/mlx.md` S-10 ("quantize the cache late, and the sink never").
- **Mechanism:** Two cheap steals from the KVQuant stack without the stack: (i) keep token 0 — in chat terms, protect the head of the system/character prompt — in FP16 and out of calibration statistics, because the attention sink is disproportionately quantisation-sensitive; (ii) at 3–4 bits, hold out a tiny sparse fraction (~1% of elements) in full precision — reported to buy more quality than an extra uniform bit. Generalised by the MLX pattern: early tokens stay full-precision and the cache converts to quantised form only past a token threshold, with group size recorded as part of the cache's identity.
- **Expected gain:** KVQuant's headline quality chain (paper-measured, WikiText-2/C4, LLaMA/Llama-2/Llama-3/Mistral): 4-bit <0.02 perplexity degradation at 3.7× cache saving; 3-bit-1% <0.1 at 4.8×; 2-bit-1% <0.5 at 6.9× — with the paper's own correction that retrieval degrades far more than perplexity suggests (RULER average 56.40 FP16 → 53.65 at 3-bit-1%, but 36.54 at 2-bit-1%). The honest operating point this evidence supports: **3-bit-class is the near-lossless floor; 2-bit is a capacity mode with named costs.** A persona fact stated once, early, is exactly a passkey — test for precisely that failure (#34).
- **Cost/risk:** The full KVQuant stack (non-uniform datatypes, pre-RoPE key caching, calibration artefacts per model) is rejected as a default — see Rejected §R6. These two steals are the parts that cost almost nothing. Pre-RoPE caching, if ever wanted, is a day-one format decision, not a retrofit.
- **Evidence: A** for the measured chain; the chat mapping of the sink rule is the dossier's stated inference.
- **Home:** KV/memory — companions to #19; the sink half can ship with #3.

### #21 — Per-layer pyramidal budgets over scored selection
- **Source:** `papers/pyramidkv.md`; `papers/snapkv.md` (the scoring it allocates).
- **Mechanism:** Uniform per-layer cache budgets waste the resource being saved: lower layers attend broadly, upper layers collapse onto a few tokens ("pyramidal information funneling"). Keep the total budget identical but distribute it as a pyramid — bottom layer at 2·k_total/m, top set by a shape parameter (paper's β = 20), arithmetic sequence between — with token selection inside each layer by SnapKV-style voting. In a paged allocator this is a per-layer block quota, not a global one: the cache manager must support a capacity *vector* from the start, even if the first policy shipped is uniform.
- **Expected gain:** Paper-measured (LongBench, 17 datasets): at comfortable budgets the win is within noise (LLaMA-3-8B average 41.49 PyramidKV vs 41.35 SnapKV at avg 2048/layer) — and at starved budgets, exactly where a 6 GB card lives, it is not: at avg 64/layer, **34.76 vs 33.05 (SnapKV), 33.89 (H2O), 30.43 (StreamingLLM)**, full cache 41.46, with TREC at 58.00 vs 38.50 for SnapKV. Do not import β = 20 blindly: it was set once, for three models, on LongBench — sweep steepness per model family on a recall-sensitive chat test first.
- **Cost/risk:** Inherits SnapKV's one-shot irreversibility; the funnel is demonstrated on LLaMA-family multi-document QA and untested on hybrids/MLA/heavily-GQA'd small models. No systems evaluation in the paper — per-layer capacities complicate tiling, unmeasured.
- **Evidence: A** for the allocation effect at equal total budget (a clean experimental design worth copying); **C** for any specific steepness on our models.
- **Home:** KV/memory — an allocation layer over #22's scoring; cheap once per-layer budgets exist.

### #22 — Per-turn re-snap: SnapKV voting as history compaction
- **Source:** `papers/snapkv.md` §7a (the adaptation is explicitly the dossier's, not the paper's).
- **Mechanism:** SnapKV proper is one-shot: at end of prefill, the last window of the prompt (the observation window) votes by attention over the prefix; keep the pooled top-k per head plus the whole window, discard the rest before generation. The chat adaptation: re-vote *per turn* over the accumulated history, using the latest user message as the observation window, with the persona/system block pinned outside the vote — SnapKV as a between-turns compaction step inside #9's composition, not a one-time gate at prefill.
- **Expected gain:** In the paper's home ground (long static prompt, single question): a ~13K-token prompt served from a 1–2K cache at small quality delta on most LongBench tasks (Mistral-7B HotpotQA 42.77 full → 40.94 at 1024, 42.32 at 4096), decode latency flat as input grows (~3.6× generation speedup and 8.2× memory efficiency vs its HuggingFace baseline at 16K input, batch 2, A100-80GB). Summarisation of long single documents is the weak spot (GovReport 27.97 → 19.79 at 1024). None of these numbers describes multi-turn chat.
- **Cost/risk:** The vote needs the observation window's attention over the prefix — the same fused-kernel exposure problem as #9. A frozen snap judges the whole future conversation by the opening exchange; the per-turn form mitigates but is untested. Selection is instruction-sensitive by design: a prompt tail that does not express the eventual task votes for the wrong prefix.
- **Evidence: A** for SnapKV as published (NeurIPS '24); **C** for the per-turn chat adaptation — flagged as ours in the source dossier and here.
- **Home:** accuracy — a compaction policy inside #9, gated by #34 on chat-shaped recall tests before it touches a live session.

### #23 — Hybrid models: per-component budgets + bounded bit-exact snapshots
- **Source:** `aesir/aesir-ideas-for-ollama.md` §3.2 (Q35-5, banked `88c71564`); `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §6; `runners/sglang.md` §5.4–§5.5 (unified tree, voting resume).
- **Mechanism:** A hybrid's inter-token memory is two things: paged KV for its attention layers, and a fixed-size recurrent (SSM/conv) state that summarises the whole prefix and cannot be prefix-reused token-wise. Budget the components separately (state bytes and snapshot counts are first-class lines, or the fit-checker lies), and make prefix reuse work by *snapshotting* the whole state at stable boundaries (end of system prompt, character card, long lore), keyed by the exact token prefix, restored bit-exact or not at all. SGLang's generalisation — one tree, a slot per component, resume from the deepest boundary every component accepts — is the policy shape to grow into.
- **Expected gain:** Our own records: snapshot restores bit-exact (state/conv/KV diffs 0.0); a stateless resend reused **72 of 90 tokens** via snapshots after residency was destroyed. Cost per snapshot for the 27B-class hybrid: ~158.9 MB SSM+conv, plus boundary KV — which is why the store is bounded (4 per session in Aesir) with a named eviction rule (keep longest prefix + most recent).
- **Cost/risk:** Snapshot placement is a policy problem, not a mechanism problem: snapshotting every token costs more than it saves. Branch costs measured on dense models must never be promised for hybrids. Applies only when hybrid GGUFs are in scope — but the budget-line discipline (#12) should land before the first hybrid arrives, not after.
- **Evidence: A** — our own campaign records with gate numbers; SGLang's productised form is project-reported (B for that part).
- **Home:** KV/memory — a branch of #5/#10 for hybrid sessions; declare `trimmable` / `prefix-rollback-able` / `quantizable-after-N` per model at load (MLX S-7) so speculation and reuse enable only where the declaration supports them.

### #24 — Cache-aware ordering of pending generations
- **Source:** `runners/sglang.md` §9.2; `papers/radixattention-sglang.md` (cache-aware scheduling, 96% of optimal hit rate as reported).
- **Mechanism:** Whenever more than one generation is pending, sort by matched prefix length descending, arrival order as tie-break: the reply (longest resident prefix) runs first and extends the tree; sidecars (summary update, memory extraction, second candidate) run against the warmed tree; all swipes of one turn run consecutively before another character's turn interleaves and thrashes a pool that holds roughly one long conversation. Engage only when queue depth exceeds one; FCFS remains the fallback default.
- **Expected gain:** Modest in steady state (a lone user rarely queues), large at the burst moments that dominate perceived quality — multi-candidate turns, app-generated sidecars, rapid chat-switching. Costs no memory and no model quality; it purely avoids evicting a prefix milliseconds before its next use.
- **Cost/risk:** Small: a matched-length tree walk per pending request (already performed at admission) plus a sort. Starvation is the fleet failure mode in miniature — hence the tie-break and a cap on how long a user-visible request may be overtaken; a cancelled high-hit request must release its pins promptly.
- **Evidence: B** — SGLang's policies and hit-rate behaviour are project/paper-reported for fleets; the single-user burst translation is the dossier's reasoning.
- **Home:** speed — a scheduler policy slice once #5 exists to be ordered against.

### #25 — Get CPU work off the decode path; profile the gap, not the average
- **Source:** `runners/sglang.md` §9.3; `memory/memory-wall-and-roofline.md` §4.3.
- **Mechanism:** Enumerate every piece of CPU work between two GPU steps — sampling bookkeeping, stop checks, detokenisation and streaming, grammar-mask preparation, KV bookkeeping, next-turn lore lookups — and get all of it off that path: run it concurrently with the in-flight forward pass (Go goroutines are a better tool for this than the Python machinery it replaces), precompute it before the loop (templates rendered, prompts tokenised while the user is still typing — idle human time is the cheapest overlap window a single-user system has), or make it cheaper. Acceptance test, copied from SGLang's method: timestamp the gaps between forward passes in a long generation; if bookkeeping gaps exceed a small fraction of step time, the loop — not the kernel — is the next target.
- **Expected gain:** At batch 1 with small-to-mid models, inter-token latency is GPU step time plus every CPU cost in the loop; shaving the CPU term is worth proportionally more the smaller the model (the same reason SGLang's overlap gain was largest on its smallest benchmark model). No number is asserted for our stack — the entry *is* the measurement discipline that produces one.
- **Cost/risk:** Pipelining hazards: a stop condition discovered in post-processing must reliably abort a prepared-but-not-yet-run step; speculative preparation must never mutate shared cache state before its step is confirmed. Goroutine-per-stage applied dogmatically to a millisecond loop can add channel hand-off latency — measure the gap, then hide what the measurement indicts; do not overlap everything.
- **Evidence: B/C** — SGLang's zero-overhead claim is project-verified by GPU trace on its stack; the Go translation is reasoned.
- **Home:** speed — a profiling-first slice; also the natural home of Aesir's dequant-placement rule (dequantise outside the per-token loop, at most once per layer per forward pass — the QS-0 cautionary tale, whose ~35× prompt-path figure is carried as briefed-but-unverified, see Open Questions).

### #26 — Small same-family draft model, only where headroom survives #3
- **Source:** `papers/speculative-decoding.md`; `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.4; `runners/ollama.md` §9 D3.
- **Mechanism:** A 0.5–1B same-family, same-tokeniser GGUF drafts γ = 3–5 tokens; the target verifies in one parallel pass under modified rejection sampling (lossless). Prefer the smallest draft that holds acceptance α — in Leviathan et al.'s sweep the smallest draft won (T5-small: 3.4× greedy on En→De; larger drafts raised α but lowered speedup). Auto-pair only drafts verified to share the target's tokeniser; a wrong-family draft actively hurts.
- **Expected gain:** Papers report 2–3× on T5-XXL (batch 1, TPU-v4) and 2–2.5× on Chinchilla-70B (distributed TPU) for the framework; the llama.cpp dossier reports 1.5–2.6× for well-matched pairs in community reports. The Ollama dossier's judgement estimate for our workload is more sober: 1.3–2× on draft-friendly (predictable, low-entropy) text, near 1× on high-entropy text. Temperature matters: expect the temp-1 band, not greedy headlines, for creative chat.
- **Cost/risk:** The draft's weights spend the scarcest resource there is. On 6 GB with a Q4 7–8B target (~4–5 GB), the remainder must hold the draft *without* evicting KV a long chat needs — a draft that forces a shorter window is a bad trade, full stop. Hence the rank: an upgrade evaluated per (target, draft) pair *after* #3 has bought the headroom, with acceptance telemetry (#15) as the gate. Draft-on-CPU is viable only for very small drafts; draft latency adds directly to every iteration.
- **Evidence: A** for the framework and its speedup law; **B** for the llama.cpp pair ranges; **C** for the local estimate.
- **Home:** speed — conditional; never the default (see Rejected §R3).

### #27 — EAGLE-3 draft head where a checkpoint exists for the exact model
- **Source:** `papers/eagle-3.md`; `papers/eagle.md` §7a.
- **Mechanism:** A single trained decoder layer (plus small fusion projections) drafts in token space, conditioned on fused low/mid/high target features and the shifted sampled tokens, trained with training-time test — unrolled multi-step training on its own outputs, which keeps acceptance nearly flat with draft depth where EAGLE-1's fell steeply. Verification is EAGLE-2's dynamic tree under the same lossless rule. The drafter reuses the target's embedding and LM head; residency for a 7B-class target is ~0.24B trained parameters (~0.5 GB FP16, ~0.25 GB Q8) plus a small draft-tree KV.
- **Expected gain:** Paper means across five tasks: **4.44× / τ 6.23** (LLaMA-Instruct 3.1 8B, temp 0) and 3.45× at temp 1 on the same target class; the 6.5× maximum is one cell (Vicuna 13B, HumanEval, temp 0) and must not be quoted as the expectation. SGLang's production check is the conservative guide for served settings: 1.81× at batch 2 down to 1.38× at batch 64 (single H100). Current llama.cpp exposes an EAGLE-3 draft path (`--spec-type draft-eagle3`) and checkpoints circulate for popular LLaMA/Qwen-family targets — alone among trained-head methods, this one is realistically reachable from a GGUF world.
- **Cost/risk:** One bespoke drafter per target model, with a larger training bill than EAGLE-1 (target-generated training data, unrolled training) — worthless for an arbitrary community model with no published checkpoint. Drafters are trained on ShareGPT/UltraChat-length dialogues; watch τ decay across a very long session's context regime. Budget rule from the dossier: if the choice is EAGLE-3 or 8K more tokens of context on 6 GB, long chat usually wants the context.
- **Evidence: A** — NeurIPS '25 paper plus an independent serving-framework evaluation; all figures datacentre hardware, batch-1 latency unless stated.
- **Home:** speed — opportunistic per-model enablement, behind the same acceptance telemetry as #15/#26.

### #28 — Embedded MTP drafting when the model ships the heads
- **Source:** `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.4 (c); `runners/ollama.md` §6.3/§9 D3; `papers/medusa.md` §7a (the native-MTP distinction).
- **Mechanism:** Where the chosen model ships multi-token-prediction tensors/heads natively, use them as the drafter (`draft-mtp` in llama.cpp terms): free drafting with no extra model to manage, no separate residency, verified by the same lossless rule as every other speculation entry.
- **Expected gain:** No corpus number isolates MTP on our workload class; the gain is whatever the shipped heads' acceptance gives at zero marginal memory — which is why it outranks its evidence: the cost side of the ratio is ~0 when the heads already exist in the file.
- **Cost/risk:** Availability is the whole story — most community GGUFs ship no such tensors. Treat as a capability check at load (like #23's declarations), never as a plan that assumes heads will appear.
- **Evidence: B** — exposed by both Ollama (draft-token controls for embedded MTP tensors) and llama.cpp as project features; unmeasured in the corpus for chat.
- **Home:** speed — a load-time capability flag beside #27.

### #29 — Tiered weight residency: hot-first placement policy
- **Source:** `runners/powerinfer.md` §8.1–§8.2.
- **Mechanism:** When a model does not fit, stop splitting *between* layers (llama.cpp `-ngl` style: some layers wholly resident, the rest wholly on CPU) and split *within* the FFN instead: fill VRAM by activation-frequency impact-per-byte — hot neuron blocks of every layer on the GPU, cold blocks computed on CPU in place — subject to a minimum-chunk rule so merge/sync cost does not eat the win. Attention and norms stay wholly on GPU (small, dense, latency-critical). The placement is computed at load from a per-model activation-frequency profile shipped as sidecar metadata, falling back to layer offload when no profile exists; hot sets are measured to be stable across tasks, so stale-profile risk is low.
- **Expected gain:** In PowerInfer's ablation, policy-over-random placement was worth ~1.5× on Falcon-40B on top of an already-hybrid engine. The dossier's speculation for 6 GB — labelled as such — is that the practical effect shows up less as peak tok/s and more as *the size of model that stays interactive* (a 13B INT4 behaving like a resident model instead of a swapped one). The full-system ceilings (up to 11.69× FP16, up to 8× INT4 on an 11 GB card) are ReLU-family numbers and must never be quoted for SwiGLU models; the SwiGLU control outcome is ~1.5× (see #30, Rejected §R1/§R9).
- **Cost/risk:** The placement policy itself is low-risk (a load-time greedy/knapsack pass, a few hundred lines). The within-FFN split is not: it needs neuron-granular kernels on both sides and a split-merge per layer per token, with a per-layer synchronisation tax that is unforgiving on slow PCIe. Prototype on one model before generalising. Profile data must be produced per model once.
- **Evidence: B/C** — SOSP '24 paper measurements for the mechanism family; the simplified-policy translation and the 6 GB expectation are the dossier's speculation.
- **Home:** KV/memory (residency policy) — relevant only for models that do not fit; irrelevant, correctly, for the resident 7–8B case #3 serves.

### #30 — Gate-first threshold sparsity for SwiGLU models (CATS)
- **Source:** `runners/powerinfer.md` §8.3 (§2.6 follow-up line).
- **Mechanism:** For SwiGLU models there are no exact zeros to exploit — but the gate projection must be computed anyway, so use it as its own predictor: calibrate a per-layer magnitude threshold offline, zero sub-threshold gate outputs, and skip the matching up/down work. No predictor weights, no training pipeline, no extra parameters. Combined with #29, the skip also halves cold-side traffic, because skipped cold neurons are precisely the ones that would have consumed host bandwidth.
- **Expected gain:** CATS reports ~50% MLP sparsity at ~1.5% quality cost with no fine-tuning, ~40% MLP-layer efficiency and ~15% end-to-end latency improvement with custom kernels in their (in-VRAM) setup. The dossier's speculation — labelled — is that the end-to-end effect should be larger in an offload setting, for the traffic reason above. Keep two execution modes and switch by phase: dense for prefill and any batched work, sparse for batch-1 decode, with a runtime fallback to dense if observed sparsity drops below threshold (PowerInfer-2's dynamic-ratio idea in policy form).
- **Cost/risk:** Threshold choice is a per-model, per-layer quality/speed dial and must be validated per model release (#34), with a conservative default. Custom-kernel work is the real cost; without kernels there is no win to collect.
- **Evidence: A/B** — CATS figures are the project's reported results as read in the dossier (paper + project summary); the offload amplification is speculation.
- **Home:** speed — pairs with #29; pointless without the split it accelerates, which is why it ranks below it.

### #31 — One residency arbiter + ordered pressure shedding
- **Source:** `runners/mlx.md` S-1, S-4, S-11 (UMA evidence, translated).
- **Mechanism:** Route weights, per-session KV and the prefix cache through one arbiter that knows the pool budget as four separate numbers (system cap, residency request, allocation budget, reusable-buffer cap) instead of one "memory used" figure. Admission may shrink the prefix cache, never the reverse by accident. On OS memory-pressure signals, shed in a fixed order: reusable buffer caches and shallow prefix snapshots at warning; the prefix cache at critical; never weights or active generations.
- **Expected gain:** The failure mode changes from "machine died under combined load" to "request queued / prefix evicted" — in the MLX field reference, warning-level shedding bought back ~1.7 GB on a 27B exactly when it mattered. On purplelongship's discrete 6 GB the arbiter's value is narrower (VRAM admission is #12's job); the transferable half is the four-number budget vocabulary and the shedding order for the host-RAM tiers (#10/#11), where model files, spill and page cache genuinely compete.
- **Cost/risk:** The largest policy-layer item on this list if built whole — deadlock and fairness questions included. Build the vocabulary and the shedding order first; the full arbiter only if measurements show independent allocators actually colliding.
- **Evidence: C** for our target — the evidence base is unified-memory systems; the dossier itself marks the full arbiter as judgement, and this ledger ranks it accordingly (real, late, and scoped).
- **Home:** robustness.

### #32 — Session-granularity draft adaptation (per-persona drafters)
- **Source:** `papers/online-speculative-decoding.md` §7a; `papers/eagle-3.md` §7b (the data-scaling convergence).
- **Mechanism:** Speculative serving already computes, at every rejection, the (context, target distribution) pair that is exactly a distillation example for the drafter's current weakness. Collect those pairs locally, and re-distil the small draft *between sessions* on the user's own verified transcripts — one adapted drafter per long-lived character/persona, selected by session (the direct descendant of the paper's per-language drafters). Verification stays exact, always: adaptation moves only acceptance α, never correctness, and a bad adaptation is visible as a speed regression, never a quality mystery. True per-8-requests online updates are over-engineering for one user; session granularity is the same idea at the right clock speed.
- **Expected gain:** Paper-measured in its native setting (serving clusters): acceptance gains of +0.1 to +0.65 across settings, 1.42×–2.17× latency reduction (final ICML figures); the thesis row is FLAN-T5-XL/Spider, where the same draft went from α 0.13 to 0.78 purely by distribution matching. A single user's character chat is about the narrowest, most persistent distribution that exists — the dossier's argument for why the local ceiling should be high. Draft residency is a non-issue (the paper's drafts are 80–160M parameters); the training footprint is not — updates belong on CPU or between sessions, never beside a 7B target on the 6 GB card during chat.
- **Cost/risk:** Real systems work: replay-buffer policy (the paper's no-eviction buffer is a prototype choice), draft versioning and swap-in, and an explicit opt-in for training on user conversations — locally stored, never leaving the machine. Sequence strictly after #15/#26 exist to be adapted.
- **Evidence: A** for the adaptation effect (ICML '24); **C** for the session-granularity local form.
- **Home:** speed — a later refinement; also the only entry that makes speculation *improve with use*, which suits a companion workload's lifetime.

### #33 — Roofline benchmarking law for the build itself
- **Source:** `memory/memory-wall-and-roofline.md` §4; `memory/mimiralloc-our-own-proven-work.md` §5.1 (measurement protocol).
- **Mechanism:** Not a feature — the discipline every other entry is graded by. Measure the card's *sustained* bandwidth (STREAM-style) on purplelongship itself and use it as the roof; count the workload's bytes (active weight bytes + KV bytes/token × context); report prefill and decode separately, decode as tok/s at a stated context and batch 1 alongside implied achieved bandwidth (tok/s × bytes/token). Medians over repeated runs, alternating-leg A/B with checksums equal, drifted runs voided openly and kept as evidence (the MimirAlloc Z-004 protocol pattern). A tok/s figure without bytes, phase, context and machine state is void on sight.
- **Expected gain:** Prevents the two classic self-deceptions: crediting an engine change for a byte-count change (a Q4-vs-FP16 comparison is primarily a bytes comparison), and chasing compute optimisations for a decode loop that sits on the bandwidth slope (at batch 1, decode intensity is ~1 FLOP/byte; the ceiling is bandwidth ÷ bytes-per-token, and only fewer bytes or more bandwidth move it). Also settles, with a number, whether a run is *at* the wall (nothing left to win) or below it by a stated gap (implementation headroom, sized).
- **Cost/risk:** Benchmarking time and the discipline to void contended runs. No product risk.
- **Evidence: A** — the method's sources are the primary literature (Wulf & McKee; McCalpin; Williams et al.) plus our own campaigns' measurement law.
- **Home:** robustness — stands from slice one; #3, #15, #19, #26, #27, #30 all report through it.

### #34 — Raw-oracle grading harness for every lossy change
- **Source:** `aesir/aesir-ideas-for-ollama.md` §6.2; `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §5.3.
- **Mechanism:** Grade every lossy or approximate change (cache precision, eviction, sparsity thresholds, typical-acceptance anything) against a raw-prompt teacher-forcing oracle: rank + logit gap per record, near-tie band (rank ≤2 with sub-logit gap) = faithful, oracle backend recorded per row, CPU engine vs CPU comparator only. Never grade against a templated CLI invocation (template rendering manufactures divergences that are in neither engine's maths — comparator artifacts are a verdict class: check template/BOS/backend/step-0 before suspecting the change). State accelerator visibility for every run; a run at 1 MiB VRAM is a CPU run and is recorded as one. Cache-precision claims additionally require a retrieval-style test (passkey/RULER-shaped) at the *longest* supported context, because that is where the KVQuant evidence shows the damage hides.
- **Expected gain:** Every other entry's quality claim becomes checkable, and every divergence gets a written verdict class or a named road — no unexplained rows. This is the harness that lets #3, #19, #20, #21, #22, #30 ship with their quality costs *known* instead of discovered by users mid-conversation.
- **Cost/risk:** One harness, plus the per-model validation time it demands. The discipline cost is refusing to ship on perplexity-at-2K alone.
- **Evidence: A** — our own Aesir campaign discipline, with its traps (the manufactured "root cause #2", the F009 hang) recorded from incidents.
- **Home:** accuracy — infrastructure, built early, used by everything lossy.

### #35 — Package-carried resource declarations + versioned defaults + auth off-loopback
- **Source:** `runners/ollama.md` §9 cross-cutting steals (§6.3, §7.5, §8.7–§8.8).
- **Mechanism:** Keep the Modelfile idea — configuration travels with the artifact — and extend the package with resource declarations (window budget, cache type, anchor policy), so a model *means* the same thing on every host instead of silently running at 4K on one site and 256K on another. Pin every default in the package or the session, and version the defaults themselves: a default change is a breaking change, announced and logged at startup and per response until acknowledged (Ollama's history shows defaults *are* API — they change behaviour under existing users). Any non-loopback bind gets authentication and TLS by default — the negative lesson from Ollama's exposure record, learned at no cost to us.
- **Expected gain:** Removes a class of portability and support failures ("why is it stupid on my machine" where the answer is a tier table nobody showed the user) and a real security exposure, for policy-level cost.
- **Cost/risk:** More package surface to version; the per-response acknowledgement must not become nagware — log it, surface it in session info (#6), move on.
- **Evidence: B/C** — the hazards are source-verified in the Ollama dossier; the declaration design is ours.
- **Home:** robustness.

### #36 — Structured-output bitmask interface (grammar compiled once)
- **Source:** `runners/vllm.md` §9.5; `runners/sglang.md` §6.4 (compressed FSM lineage); `papers/radixattention-sglang.md` (FSM side result: 1.6× throughput on its JSON benchmark).
- **Mechanism:** Constrain sampling by masking disallowed logits to −∞ after the forward pass, with the grammar compiled once up front. Steal the *interface* (bitmask in, masked logits out) — a clean separation that composes with speculation and the token-budget step loop — without importing a grammar-compiler dependency (XGrammar-class) until a real requirement sizes it.
- **Expected gain:** Guaranteed-shape output (JSON/tool calls) without post-hoc repair loops, for the workloads that need it; none for free-form character chat, which is why this ranks last despite being well-evidenced elsewhere.
- **Cost/risk:** Mask preparation must live off the decode critical path (#25) or it taxes every token to serve an occasional need.
- **Evidence: B** — standard in both fleet engines, project-reported; the interface-only scope is our judgement.
- **Home:** accuracy — opportunistic; build when a consumer (tool-calling, structured memory extraction sidecars) actually lands.

## 4. Top five — scheduling reasoning for the coordinator

The top five are not simply the five largest wins. They are the five that make the rest of the ledger possible, in dependency order. A coordinator should read the sequence, not just the set.

**#2 Pinned anchor + #6 Loud disclosure — schedule first, together, as policy.** Neither needs new kernels, an allocator, or a cache. Together they convert the build's founding law (the anchor cannot be pushed out) from an intention into a tested property, and they install the observability (reuse counts, eviction reports, effective-precision reporting) that every later slice will be graded through. Shipping anything lossy before #6 exists means shipping it unmeasured in production. Cost: days, not slices. This pair is also the only top-five work that is pure win with no quality trade-off to validate.

**#1 Resident sessions — the object everything else attaches to.** Prefix caching (#5), persistence (#10), stable-order injection (#14), the post-history anchor (#7) and per-persona drafters (#32) are all, structurally, properties of a session object. Building any of them against the stateless API first means building it twice. HS-6's lesson generalises: token-level residency (the committed-token watermark) must be in the session's first design, because the generation-seam loss it eliminates is invisible until real resends hit it. Sequence: session object with watermark first, composition (anchor segments, injection) second, retention third.

**#3 q8_0 KV default — the arithmetic that decides what the sessions can hold.** This is a policy change over existing cache types, so it can land in parallel with #1/#2 — but it must land *before* #9 (eviction scoring) and before #10's persistence format freezes, because both inherit the cache precision: scoring operates on the cache this entry sizes, and a saved session is only valid for the precision it was saved at (hence the envelope). Its worked case is the build's founding arithmetic: on this card, f16 does not meet even the 8K minimum for an 8B chat (4,912 tokens), and q8_0 does (9,246). Validate per model family through #34 as it lands, not after.

**#4 Block allocation — the substrate.** Radix/hash prefix retention (#5), block-granular eviction (#9), spill and persistence (#10/#11), per-layer quotas (#16/#21) and hybrid snapshots (#23) are all operations *on blocks*. Without the arena + block table + reference counts, each would invent its own allocation shape and the campaign would pay five integrations instead of one. This is the deepest surgery in the five and the one to staff most carefully; its acceptance test is memory honesty (exact KV accounting, teardown returning provably to baseline — the MimirAlloc L-006 lifecycle shape), not speed.

**#5 Prefix cache (trunk + branches) — the retention layer, last of the five by dependency, first by felt impact.** It needs #4's blocks to share, #1's sessions to own the sharing, #13's rendering discipline to hit, and #3's precision to afford keeping prefixes resident at all. Once it lands, the workload's economics invert: later turns cost the new turn only, swipes cost a suffix, and #10's tiering has something worth tiering. The dependency the brief names — *prefix caching before tiering* — is exactly this: tiering (#10/#11) is a policy over retained prefix objects, and scheduling it earlier produces a persistence format with nothing stable to persist. The second named dependency — *q8_0 KV before eviction scoring* — is #3 before #9, for the same reason: do not tune a scoring policy against a cache whose size is about to halve.

**What the five deliberately exclude:** all speculation (#15/#26/#27). Speculation multiplies decode speed on a foundation that must first be correct, resident and honest; n-gram speculation (#15) is cheap enough to add the moment the decode loop stabilises, and the trained/draft forms are per-model opportunities after that. A coordinator tempted to lead with EAGLE-3's headline numbers should re-read its entry: those numbers are real, and they are worth nothing to a user whose persona was truncated at token 5.

## 5. Rejected / deferred — priced by the corpus, do not re-litigate

Each entry names the idea, where the corpus priced it, and the reason it is wrong *for this target*. "Rejected" means do not build it for the 6 GB single-user build; "deferred" means a named condition would reopen it.

**R1. Learned activation predictors (DejaVu-style) for SwiGLU sparsity — REJECTED.**
Priced in `runners/powerinfer.md` §8.4: a per-layer MLP predictor at the paper's ~7% parameter ratio is ~0.5B parameters for a 7B-class model — ~0.5 GB at INT8, ~1 GB at FP16, resident in a 6 GB budget — plus two small matmuls per layer per token on the critical path, plus a per-model training pipeline (hours per model) that a Go project does not otherwise need. Modern chat models are SwiGLU: there are no exact zeros, and the learned predictor is the part of PowerInfer that does not survive contact with a small-VRAM, no-training-pipeline product. The free alternative exists and is ranked instead: gate-first thresholding (#30). A narrow low-rank gate approximator is the only form that could reopen this, and only after #30 is exhausted.

**R2. Datacentre preemption-by-recompute as the *sole* overflow policy — REJECTED (as a sole policy).**
vLLM V1 recomputes because, in a datacentre, recompute is cheaper than many small PCIe swap transfers. For a single long chat the victim is the user's whole conversation: re-prefilling 32K tokens on a laptop GPU is minutes, paid by the person waiting on exactly that conversation. `runners/vllm.md` §9.4 and `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §4.3 both invert the policy locally: spill idle sessions to RAM/disk, recompute only short spans, keep recompute as the always-working fallback. Recompute as *fallback* is in the ledger (#11); recompute as *doctrine* is rejected.

**R3. Draft-GGUF speculation as the *default* speculation — REJECTED as default (kept as #26, conditional).**
The draft model spends the scarcest resource on the card — VRAM — to buy speed, and on 6 GB it competes byte-for-byte with the KV cache a long chat needs. Meanwhile the zero-weight member of the same family (n-gram/prompt-lookup, #15) captures the repetitive-chat win for megabytes, losslessly, today. A default that evicts context to fund a drafter is the wrong trade for this workload; a per-pair, telemetry-gated option is the right one. Do not re-litigate the default; litigate individual (target, draft) pairs with acceptance data.

**R4. Medusa-style bolt-on heads for arbitrary GGUFs — DEFERRED (blocked on artefacts that do not exist).**
Medusa's residency is the best in the trained family (no draft model; a few hundred MB of heads for a 7B-class target) — and irrelevant in practice: heads must be trained per target model on the unquantised backbone's hidden states, community GGUFs ship without them, and llama.cpp's shipped speculation is draft-model and n-gram based. Two further cautions from `papers/medusa.md`: its default *typical acceptance* rule does **not** preserve the output distribution (only rejection sampling is lossless — any deployment promising "same output, faster" must use it and accept the lower speedup), and Medusa-2 changes the backbone, i.e. it is a fine-tune with a fine-tune's regression burden. Reopens only if the build campaign ships its own model releases, where Medusa-1-style heads baked in at fine-tune time are the cheapest integrated drafter available.

**R5. Full Jacobi Lookahead decoding — REJECTED as an implementation target (idea already captured by #15).**
Lookahead spends the scarcest thing on small hardware — FLOPs: its tuned A100 configurations imply 56–120× extra FLOPs per step, absorbed only while decoding is bandwidth-bound with compute to spare. The paper's own RTX 3090 result (~1.3× on MT-Bench) is the realistic reference class for consumer cards, its acceptance length (τ ≈ 1.5–2.0) is the lowest in the speculation wave, and its fused lookahead+verification step needs a bespoke attention mask per backend. llama.cpp's n-gram modes are mechanically its pool-and-verify idea with table-lookup drafting instead of extra model FLOPs, and they dominate it on 6 GB. Trajectory-mined n-grams (harvesting discarded-but-computed continuations into the pool) is the one refinement worth remembering — as a #15 extension, not a project.

**R6. Full KVQuant as the default KV scheme — REJECTED as default (two steals extracted as #20).**
Five interacting mechanisms, per-model calibration artefacts (Fisher-weighted codebooks, key scales, thresholds) that arbitrary community GGUFs do not ship, CPU-offloaded threshold computation, and pre-RoPE key caching that is incompatible with every existing engine's cache format and with any persisted prefix store built on post-RoPE keys. Each mechanism's individual gain in the paper's own chain is modest (0.19–0.82 perplexity); the stack pays off only whole, and whole is a research project. The near-free parts — sink/head FP16 protection and 1% outlier sparing — are in the ledger. Reopens only for a small set of first-class models the campaign itself calibrates and ships.

**R7. KIVI-2 (2-bit) as an unvalidated default — DEFERRED behind a per-model gate.**
The asymmetric scheme is ranked (#19); the 2-bit *default* is not. The paper's own evidence bounds it: LongBench capped at 4,096 tokens for most models (nothing validates 2-bit at 32K+), and Falcon/MQA — the already-head-compressed architectures — needed 4-bit to hold accuracy. On a card whose entire purpose is long context, shipping the least-validated-at-long-context precision as the default inverts the evidence. 2-bit is a labelled capacity mode per model, after #34 grading at the longest supported context.

**R8. Non-prefix modular reuse (Prompt Cache splicing, CacheBlend fusion) as early work — DEFERRED behind a quality gate.**
Both solve a real chat problem (lore chunks assembled in varying order never form a clean prefix) and both are approximate in ways prefix reuse is not: Prompt Cache rests on empirical tolerance of discontinuous position IDs, with per-dataset accuracy movement in both directions in its own table; CacheBlend's ~15% selective-recompute figure comes from FP/8-bit datacentre models on QA/summarisation and must be re-measured for roleplay models and quantised weights, and its HKVD fusion needs layer-wise partial-prefill hooks inside the engine — real work, not a flag. The cheap intermediate (chunk-level KV persistence + plain prefix reuse) is already #10. Reopens when measurements show lore re-prefill dominating TTFT *after* #5/#10 land.

**R9. PowerInfer's full sparse-hybrid engine, and ReLUfied model catalogues — REJECTED for this build.**
The headline numbers (up to 11.69× FP16, up to 8× INT4, 11 GB card) belong to ReLU-family models. The SwiGLU control outcome in the same dossier is ~1.5×. Building neuron-granular hybrid kernels to chase the ReLU ceiling on a SwiGLU catalogue misprices the work by an order of magnitude; shipping converted/ReLUfied model variants to manufacture the ceiling makes the runner own a model-quality liability (the conversions carry multi-billion-token fine-tunes and their own quality caveats). What survives — placement policy (#29), gate-first sparsity (#30), the LRU neuron cache and cluster-pipeline shapes if a flash/RAM weight tier ever exists — is already extracted.

**R10. A custom general allocator (MimirAlloc included) for the KV pool or for decode speed — REJECTED, on our own measurements.**
This is the rejection our own records argue loudest. MimirAlloc — correct, fuzzed, soaked, 117/117 on two campaigns — lost to glibc on every general workload on the Pi (5.4×–65.2× in the K-002 baseline) and on most core shapes on the Z13, including **all three KV-cache-shaped probes (Z-115, no adoption)**. Decode is bandwidth-bound, not malloc-bound. The pool is an arena with a free list (#4); a general allocator is for the structures around it. Where allocator work could still pay is footprint/longevity (retention: back to within 128 KiB of baseline after free, where glibc retained 1,232 KiB, Pi K-008) — a server-hygiene property to *measure* (#33), not a reason to adopt an allocator. Any future proposal carries the burden of a K-002-style A/B on the target machine.

**R11. Fleet serving machinery: continuous batching as a throughput project, multi-process engine cores, CUDA graphs, disaggregated prefill/decode, tensor/pipeline parallelism — REJECTED for this target.**
Orca's 36.9× is a static-batching comparison on GPT-3 175B; with one user and usually one active generation there is no batch to continuously refill, and the PagedAttention paper's 2–4× scales with the concurrency we do not have. What transfers from that world is already extracted: the iteration-level step loop (#18/#25), the token-budget scheduler shape, block allocation (#4). The rest solves Python contention, multi-GPU communication and fleet fairness problems a single-user Go binary does not have. (`runners/vllm.md` §9.5 states the same list as its own "what not to steal".)

**R12. Forking ggml/libllama; exposing llama-server directly; the fixed-slot scheduler as a concurrency story — REJECTED.**
`runners/llamacpp-ggml.md` §8.7, with Ollama's 2025–2026 engine detour as the controlled experiment: subprocess + pinned upstream build + compatibility translation is the proven pattern; a fork inherits the whole maintenance surface for no workload-relevant gain. Exposing llama-server beyond loopback imports its unauthenticated, unnamespaced session surface into our product. And if multi-user serving ever becomes a goal, it should be entered deliberately with paged-KV/prefix-tree designs (#4/#5), not by stretching slots.

**R13. "Infinite memory" readings of StreamingLLM — REJECTED as a claim (the policy is #9).**
Sink+window gives stable, constant-cost generation over unbounded *input*; it provides no recall of anything evicted, and the paper says so. Any build or UI copy that lets "infinite chat" imply "remembers everything" is a dishonesty defect: the character has forgotten whatever scrolled out, and the product must pair the policy with an explicit memory mechanism (#8) and say which is in force. Recorded here so the marketing version is never re-litigated as if it were the technique.

**R14. UMA retention breadth imported wholesale to a discrete card — REJECTED (translation, not transfer).**
MLX's lesson that "keeping a cache is cheaper than recomputing it" (S-9) is priced in unified memory, where a dormant cache costs only its bytes and never left the pool. On a 6 GB discrete card, resident KV is priced against the weights themselves; retention breadth must scale with the pool, and #3's arithmetic — not UMA instinct — sets it. The parts of the MLX dossier that *do* transfer (budget vocabulary, shedding order, capability declarations) are in #23/#31.

## 6. Open questions — what the corpus could not settle

The build campaign's own measurements should answer these, in roughly the order they will bite. Each names its source dossier and the cheapest settling experiment the corpus suggests.

1. **The true runtime reserve on the RTX 2060 Max-Q.** kv-cache-design §7.2's 512 MiB is a planning figure, flagged as such; the procedure's outputs move by ±1 precision tier if the true reserve differs by ~±300 MiB. *Settle by:* a measured reserve per backend at first implementation (weights + cache + scratch + driver overhead on the actual card). This gates #3's shipped `num_ctx`.
2. **Sustained memory bandwidth of purplelongship's RTX 2060 Max-Q.** No record available to the corpus measures it, so no tok/s figure for the card is asserted anywhere in it. *Settle by:* the STREAM-style measurement #33 prescribes, before any speed claim. Gates every speed entry's local expectation.
3. **N-gram acceptance on real character-chat transcripts.** Both speculation dossiers flag it: acceptance on repetitive chat will differ sharply from benchmarks, in an unknown direction per persona. *Settle by:* instrument α on exported real transcripts (the telemetry #15 mandates) before sizing #26/#27 investments.
4. **Speculation × parallel slots.** The `--parallel 1` restriction is asserted by multiple secondary guides but not stated in the llama.cpp server README the dossier read — unverified against source. *Settle by:* checking the target pinned build's behaviour before shipping #15's disable-cleanly logic.
5. **The KV-quantisation × Flash Attention coupling matrix.** Stated as a prerequisite from two independent sources; the exact per-backend/per-type matrix was not read from primary source (partially verified, llamacpp dossier §9). *Settle by:* verifying on our own build, per backend, and reporting the *effective* cache type in force (#3/#6) — a fork-point check, in the Ollama dossier's phrase.
6. **Can the runner accumulate attention scores cheaply under fused attention?** #9 (H2O scoring) and #22 (SnapKV voting) both need per-token attention sums that FlashAttention-style kernels do not materialise. The dossiers argue a newest-query-only reduction is O(context) and small; nobody in the corpus measured it on this stack. *Settle by:* a kernel spike with the reduction as a fused epilogue, costed against the cache saving it buys. This is the single largest technical unknown in the ledger.
7. **Per-turn re-snap quality (our adaptation, not SnapKV's result).** Re-voting per turn with the latest message as observation window is the corpus's invention; its recall behaviour across topic changes mid-chat is unmeasured anywhere. *Settle by:* #34 grading on chat-shaped recall tests (needle facts planted early, queried after topic pivots) before it touches a live session.
8. **Pyramid steepness per model family.** β = 20 was set once, for three models, on LongBench; hybrids, MLA and heavily-GQA'd small models are untested for the funnel itself. *Settle by:* logging per-layer attention entropy / top-k mass during prefill per onboarded model and deriving its actual funnel shape (the PyramidKV dossier's Aesir suggestion, applied here), then a small steepness sweep.
9. **Aggressive KV precision at 32K+.** KIVI's evaluation caps at 4–8K for most models; KVQuant validates quality to 32K and shows retrieval (RULER) degrading far faster than perplexity at 2-bit. Nothing in the corpus validates *any* sub-8-bit scheme at the contexts #19/#20 would be used for. *Settle by:* #34's rule — retrieval grading at the longest supported context, per model, before a precision tier is labelled for that context.
10. **Ollama-line unknowns inherited by any fork decision.** Engine routing per model family at v0.40.x; whether the measured-placement scheduler (§5.4) survived the return to llama-server intact; MLX-path prefix reuse and truncation behaviour — all recorded as *not fully verified* in `runners/ollama.md`. *Settle by:* direct checks against the pinned build the build campaign actually forks from, before #12/#17 designs are finalised against assumed behaviour.
11. **Block size for a single-sequence pool.** PagedAttention's 16 is an A100 multi-request measurement; the vLLM dossier argues 32–64 for our shape; kv-cache-design defaults to 16 with a sharing-shape caveat. The corpus disagrees with itself, honestly. *Settle by:* a small sweep on the target (tail waste vs table/hash work vs sub-block sharing frequency) during #4's implementation.
12. **The Aesir QS-0 per-step dequant figure (~35×).** Carried in the Aesir dossier as briefed-but-not-verified: the VM-side records name the QS-0 baseline and its indictments but do not reproduce the number. *Settle by:* confirming against `QS-0-BASELINE.md` on purpleraider (branch `yrsa/hip-serve`, commit `bad65106`) before quoting it outward. The design rule it supports (dequantise outside the per-token loop, #25) stands on the mechanism regardless of the exact factor.
13. **CacheBlend's ~15% recompute ratio on roleplay models and quantised weights.** Measured on FP/8-bit datacentre models for QA/summarisation (R8's reopening condition). *Settle by:* re-measuring the ratio's quality knee on a persona+lore workload if and when R8 reopens — not before.
14. **Hybrid geometry for models the corpus could not census.** kv-cache-design §6.2's qwen35 attention interval (how many of its 64 blocks are full-attention) is unstated in the Aesir records read; the dossier gives the exact per-layer rate and an explicitly labelled illustrative total instead. *Settle by:* one GGUF metadata read per hybrid model at onboarding — which #12's `kv_rate`-from-metadata rule makes routine.
